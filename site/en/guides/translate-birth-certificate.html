Preparing and printing a birth certificate translation

A practical sequence for comparing the original, entering Korean text, checking dates, saving a PDF and signing the printout.

Written and reviewed by MYDY · Updated October 29, 2026

On this page1. Identify the document2. Enter names, places and identifiers3. Check dates and long entries4. Save, print and signBefore submission

1. Identify the document

First check that the original includes parent information. The public translation tool follows an Alberta layout; do not force a certificate from another province into that format. The Korean translation and the Korean birth-registration application are different documents.

Keep the original beside you and match one field at a time. Compare names, identifiers and dates directly instead of filling them from memory. Examples use fictional names such as 김하늘 and 김예시, never real family records.

2. Enter names, places and identifiers

Enter the child’s Korean surname in Surname and the remaining names in Given Names. For 김하늘, enter 김 and 하늘 separately. Type the Korean pronunciation of the English registrar name yourself; there are no automatic name suggestions.

Check and transcribe the birth city into Korean. In the Alberta form, keep the mother’s and father’s names and birth countries in the correct fields. The registration number and certificate number near the bottom may differ; locate each on the original rather than duplicating one number.

3. Check dates and long entries

The birth, registration and issue dates mean different things. Check the month/day order before choosing calendar dates. The tool validates real calendar dates and the order birth ≤ registration ≤ issue. If validation fails, recheck the source fields rather than inventing a date to pass the check.

Long names or birthplaces may use smaller text in the PDF. Do not arbitrarily shorten required information. Check readability in the preview and, if needed, ask the operator using fictional examples before printing.

4. Save, print and sign

  1. Enter the translator’s name and open the preview.
  2. Compare the translation with the original from top to bottom. The completion counter is not an accuracy score.
  3. Download the PDF and reopen the saved file.
  4. Select Letter paper (8.5 × 11 inches) and check the print preview for clipping.
  5. Sign by hand at the (인) mark beside the translator’s name.

If the font looks broken or the preview is blank, check the connection and try opening the downloaded file in a separate PDF viewer. For failed downloads, check browser download-blocking notices and storage permissions. Refreshing clears the form, so review any entries you need before doing so.

Before submission

Check names, dates, numbers, issuer wording, the translator’s name and the handwritten signature. Follow the official consular instructions (Korean) for current requirements and handling of original documents. This is a tool guide, not a determination that a particular document will be accepted.
